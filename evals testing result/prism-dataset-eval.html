What the results say

The reviewer's reading of the numbers below.

  • 1. With the app's default model, 12 of 27 datasets produced nothing, and 3 more produced only 1 or 2 documents. The generator was openai/gpt-4o-mini, the default the web app uses when a user does not pick a model (api/engine.py). The main cause is the app's own length rule: the model writes shorter documents than D04, D05, D07, D19, D23 and D26 require (D07 needs at least 1,700 words, D04 and D05 at least 850). D10 and D13 were rejected for missing their core structure (too few key-value entries; not enough entries sharing a name). D06 used its whole 300-call budget on retries. D08 and D12 made 2 documents, and D24 made 1. In the earlier run with gpt-5-mini, 23 of 27 generated, so the default model choice decides most of this.
  • 2. The app gives users the wrong reason when a dataset fails. D21 told the user 'the API key was rejected', and D25 called it a provider problem, yet the model returned 69,032 and 70,473 tokens of output with the same working key. The error classifier (utils/reliability/provider_errors.py) looks for words like '401', 'authentication' and 'limit exceeded' anywhere in an error's text. Tested directly: a JSON error at 'column 401' and any error quoting text with the word 'authentication' are both reported as 'API key was rejected'. D21's documents are about software releases and contain 'authentication'. D21, D22 and D25 all ended with 'generation returned empty' although the model had written 69,000 to 94,000 tokens: their output was produced and then thrown away, most likely at parsing.
  • 3. D20 still rewards a system that refuses everything, and some of its 'unanswerable' answers are wrong. All 22 D20 questions have the answer 'The passage does not state this' and none is an answerable control, so refusing every question scores 100%. The earlier fix keeps control questions when they are generated; this model generated none. Checked by hand in D20_0002: 'What year did the San Francisco earthquake occur?' is keyed as not stated, but the text says 1906; 'What was the date of the Great Lisbon Earthquake?' is keyed as not stated, but the text says 1 November 1755. The judge rated only 36% of D20 answers correct.
  • 4. D14 copies the example IDs from its own prompt into every document. All four D14 documents use PN#7734-88A-0012, SN-QX4471-0098-EU and LOT-2231-B-0447, the examples written into utils/core/prompts/d14_rare_tokens.py (lines 42 to 65), instead of inventing their own. Across the dataset the same question now has conflicting answers: 'What happened to PN#7734-88A-0012?' has four different keys, and 'On what date was LOT-2231-B-0447 released?' is answered '12 January 2023' in one document and 'passed all quality assurance checks' in another. For a dataset about unique identifiers this defeats the test.
  • 5. Many questions can be answered without the document. With no document at all, the reader answered correctly on D02 (88%), D27 (85%), D01 (72%), D03 (65%) and D11 (56%). Those questions test what a model already knows, not retrieval. Part of this is how the answers are written: long explanatory answers (D02, D27) are easier to match loosely, so treat the exact numbers as approximate. D09, D16, D17 and D18 do well here (5% to 11%).
  • 6. Internal labels reach the files users download. D03 documents carry [chunk_001]-style labels, and all 20 D03 answers cite them ('chunk 002'). That hands a RAG system the chunk boundaries D03 exists to test. D01 documents keep [fact_001] [DEFINITION] tags on every line; the code adds them on purpose (utils/core/llm_content_generator.py:643), but they are answer-key labels in the text a user ingests.
  • 7. Answers too long to grade. The project's own rule is that an answer over 30 words cannot be used as a grading key. Every D02 answer breaks it (64 to 91 words), as does every D27 answer (41 to 73) and most D03 answers (25 to 44).
  • 8. Control questions and question types still go missing. D17 produced no same-language control questions and D20 no answerable controls, so neither has the easy counterpart its scoring needs. D11 has no select_function questions. Four of five D11 documents are about the same subject (Python duration-parsing utilities), and D02 and D27 each repeat a topic twice.
  • 9. What works. D09 (tables) and D16 (style shift) are the closest to good: built the way they must be, answers right, low scores without the document (9% and 11%) and high with it (91% and 89%). Only a missing document or two pairs kept them from passing. D15 and D17 also separate the two readers well (95% vs 32%, 100% vs 7%). Topics suited their dataset in every document judged.
  • 10. About the judges. The first judge (gemini-2.5-flash-lite) also graded the reader's answers and marked correct answers wrong, for example 'use read_file_utf16, it returns the content as a string' against the key read_file_utf16('example.txt'). Judging and grading were redone with gemini-2.5-flash. The two judges then disagreed on whether documents were 'built right'. Checked by hand: the second judge was right about D20 (its unanswerable questions are answered in the text), and the main judge was right about D11 (parse_duration and parse_duration_precise are the look-alike pair D11 needs). The report shows both, so treat that column as a lead, not a verdict.

How to read this report

Each dataset is meant to expose one weakness in a RAG system. A dataset is only useful if its documents really contain that trap, its answers are right, and its questions cannot be passed without doing the work. The columns test exactly that.

  • Auto checks: share of Q&A pairs with no mechanical problem: the question type is one the dataset declares, the answer's words appear in the document, the quoted context is in the document, the answer is short enough to grade (30 words), no duplicate question, no leftover markers or chat text.
  • Topic fits / Built right / Answers right: the AI judge (google/gemini-2.5-flash, a different model family from the generator) read each document with the dataset's own definition. "Built right" means the document contains the dataset's specific construction, not just a generic article. "Answers right" counts only fully correct answers. Bar: 80%.
  • With doc: a reader model (openai/gpt-4o-mini) answered every question holding the whole document. This is the ceiling. Below 80% means the answer key or the questions have problems; below 50% makes the dataset broken.
  • Without doc: the same reader with no document. Above 40% means many questions can be answered from general knowledge, so they do not test retrieval.
  • Refuse-all: a system that answers every question with "The document does not say." It should score near zero, except on D20, where some questions are meant to be declined (bar 60%).
  • Grading: a declined answer is judged by rule; every other answer is compared with the answer key by the judge model, which also notes when the reader gave the dataset's planted wrong answer.
  • Verdict: Broken if fewer than 3 documents or 40% of pairs were made, or the reader with the document scores under 50%. Otherwise Needs work if any bar above is missed, a declared question type or control question is missing, or more than 20% of pairs fail automatic checks.

Limits. Five documents per dataset is a small sample: one bad document moves a percentage by 20 points. The judge and reader are cheap models and make mistakes of their own, so treat single verdicts as leads and the patterns as the result. Spot-check two documents per dataset against the judge (the files are listed in each section).
